NHL Final Four Blueprint: Performance, Not Size, Defines Deep Playoff Runs

The four remaining teams share a clear on-ice profile: they control the scoring battle, limit opponents' best scoring chances, get above-average goaltending and create more takeaways. But their roster profiles are less uniform, suggesting there is no single age-or-size formula behind a deep run.

Key findings

Deep playoff runs are being defined by execution

The clearest pattern is not that the final four are built the same way. It is that they are beating the playoff field in the same repeatable areas on the ice.

1

The final four share a clear performance profile

  • Carolina, Colorado, Vegas and Montreal all beat the playoff-team average in seven shared performance metrics, suggesting their common thread is execution rather than roster type.
7 shared metrics
2

Seven metrics show the shared blueprint

  • All four teams were above the playoff average for share of total goals, goals scored per game, goals allowed per game, fewer quality chances allowed from dangerous areas, fewer dangerous shots allowed, save percentage and takeaways per game.
7 shared metrics
3

Those metrics sit under five traits

  • The seven shared metrics point to five broader performance traits: scoring control, defensive strength, limiting opponents' best scoring chances, goaltending and puck pressure. Put simply, the final four are scoring more, conceding less, keeping opponents away from the best shooting areas, getting saves and winning pucks back.
5 performance traits
4

There is no single roster-build formula

  • The roster profile is less consistent: three of the four teams are shorter and lighter than the playoff-team average, while only Montreal is younger. That makes the performance trend stronger than any age-or-size explanation.

Methodology

How the analysis works

This analysis compares the four remaining teams with the wider playoff field to identify what they have in common.

  • Performance data: MoneyPuck playoff team data was used across all situations.
  • Comparison group: each final-four team was measured against the average of all playoff teams in the dataset.
  • Shared metrics: a metric is counted as a shared final-four trait only when all four teams were better than the playoff-team average.
  • Roster data: age, height and weight were added from NHL roster data to check whether the teams also shared a similar physical profile.
  • Interpretation: the analysis is descriptive and does not claim that any single roster trait caused playoff success.
